Governing legacy mainframe COBOL data for a tier 1 health insurer

How we took a tier 1 health insurer from zero governance to full lineage visibility across every legacy mainframe COBOL copybook.

By Mohak Jani · Nov 11, 2026

Picture one of the largest health insurers in the U.S., generating tens of billions of dollars in revenue a year, with decades of data still living in mainframe COBOL files. That data flows into a modern HDFS landing zone, and until recently nobody was governing the path between the two. This is the story of how we changed that, and it is written for data leaders who suspect they have the same blind spot.

Key takeaways

  • We took the insurer from zero governance to full lineage visibility across every COBOL copybook.
  • The catalog had to be configured and proven first; the mainframe scanning came second.
  • Scheduling, a visible UI, knowledge transfer and managed services are what made it production-live.

Where we started

When we arrived, the customer's Informatica Enterprise Data Catalog was installed but unconfigured. It had never been smoke-tested and was not in production use. Meanwhile, the legacy COBOL files feeding the HDFS landing zone had no governance controls at all, so a critical data path was effectively unmonitored.

Two more things made it harder:

  • Unsupported scanning. The legacy mainframe file system fell outside standard metadata scanning support, which left COBOL copybook structures invisible to the catalog.
  • Manual tracking. Changes to legacy COBOL metadata were tracked by hand in documentation, creating discovery gaps and real audit and financial risk.

Listening first

Before touching the environment, we talked to people. We interviewed the major stakeholders to understand what had already been done with the catalog and what they expected from a governance program, and we carried out a technical assessment of the existing Informatica and mainframe environment.

Everything went into one consolidated requirements and assessment document, which we reviewed and validated with the stakeholders before implementation began. That review gave everyone a shared, written picture of the starting point, so the build work that followed was aimed at agreed needs rather than assumptions.

Getting the foundation working

With requirements agreed, we built the catalog up from the ground up. We configured and smoke-tested Informatica EDC 10.1, then established and validated the mainframe source connection against the customer's COBOL file systems.

On the target side, we configured the HDFS landing zone on the Big Data Management / Big Data Engineering (BDE) 10.1 environment, fixing issues as we found them. Only once the pieces were proven did we move on to the part the catalog could not do by itself.

Teaching the catalog to read COBOL

Because standard scanning did not cover the mainframe file system, we developed custom COBOL copybook scanners. We built them on the customer's existing code, together with Informatica Data Processor (Data Transformation).

We integrated the scanners with EDC so that copybook structures could be catalogued and searched like any other governed asset, then loaded the scan results into the EDC HDFS landing zone to feed downstream lineage and impact analysis.

Field note

The goal was never a separate tool for legacy data. It was for a COBOL copybook to be as easy to find and trace as any other asset in the catalog.

Making it part of the way they work

A governance capability only helps if it runs without anyone remembering to run it. We integrated the solution with the broader Informatica platform and with Control-M, the corporate scheduler, and exposed scan results, lineage and copybook relationships in the EDC user interface.

We then performed QA, supported go-live, delivered knowledge transfer to the customer's team, and continued with post-production managed services. Handover and ongoing support were part of the engagement from the start, not an afterthought, so the customer's team could own what we built.

Where they ended up

The customer now has a fully functional, production-live governance environment. It gives complete lineage and impact visualization of COBOL copybooks, and it discovers metadata changes automatically instead of relying on hand-maintained documentation. We estimate the benefit at millions of dollars per year in avoided audit penalties and customer churn.

You can read the full engagement in our case study.

If this sounds familiar

If legacy mainframe data feeds your modern platforms and your catalog cannot see it, start by listing which of those sources your catalog can actually scan today. Our advisory and assessment team can help you scope the gap, and our managed services team can keep the solution running once it is live.
